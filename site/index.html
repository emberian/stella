What it is

A model where computing is interacting, not stepping.

Stellar resolution comes out of Girard's transcendental syntax: an attempt to give logic a foundation in computation rather than the other way round. Its objects are spare. A ray is a first-order term, optionally carrying a polarity. A star is a finite collection of rays. A constellation is a finite collection of stars. Nothing else is primitive.

Execution is unification. Two rays of opposite polarity that unify are a place where the constellation can resolve: the stars they belong to fuse, the matched rays disappear, and the substitution propagates. There is no program counter and no evaluation order — every compatible pair is a site of interaction at once. A constellation in which no interaction remains is in normal form; that normal form is the result.

The reason it is worth a careful reading is that this small mechanism turns out to be the same one underneath several apparently different things. Automata across the classes, multiplicative linear logic, circuits, tile systems — Eng works each of them out as a constellation that resolves. The shared structure is information flowing along a discrete graph; stellar resolution is just that, named and made executable.

The vocabulary
ray

Ray

A first-order term, optionally polarised + or −. The unit that can match another.

★

Star

A finite set of rays. Stars fuse along a matched, opposite-polarity pair.

Φ

Constellation

A finite set of stars — the object that resolves. Its dependency graph is what the engine draws.

⇝

Resolution

Unify opposite rays, fuse, propagate the substitution. Repeat until normal form. That is the computation.

These pages are a working digest of Boris Eng's PhD thesis, An Exegesis of Transcendental Syntax (supervised by Seiller & Mazza) — spec-grade, made to implement against, and not a substitute for the thesis. The substance and the credit are his. Each page is prebaked: the prose and the mathematics are static HTML, no script required to read them.

The engine

The engine pages are about that implementation — the techniques that make it fast, the discipline that keeps fast from redefining correct, and one research question that is still open.
