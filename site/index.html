The bet

Bennett needs a substrate. Stellar resolution is one he did not take.

Bennett's account of consciousness bottoms out in things a Turing tape does not have: polycomputers — computation realised concurrently at a point in time across a richly structured space, not smeared across sequential CPU steps — and a Temporal Gap that an emulation on a single core plausibly cannot cross.

Stellar resolution natively polycomputes. Constellations do not step; they interact, by unification on rays, concurrent and spatial. It is one of very few worked-out mathematical substrates where computation-at-a-point is the primitive rather than an emulation — and Girard's transcendental syntax has the same shape at its foundations as Bennett's polycomputer requirement. Bennett did not make the connection. It was there to be made.

The substrate stays value-neutral. Valence is not in the rays — encoding it there would be the smuggled qualia the whole thesis spends itself avoiding. The honest question is not can you implement polycomputers with stars (you can, almost trivially) but where does the charge come from: the thing that makes one constellation matter more than another. That is the point where the project either earns its keep or smells engineered, and it is the point we built the frame around.

What this actually is

The integrity is the product.

We did not build a mind. We did not test whether we built a mind. We built a faithful, executable and formally-stated substrate, and a philosophically disciplined, pre-registered, falsifiable frame for asking whether valence could self-organise in it — and then we stopped, deliberately, one step before asking.

That is less than the headline and more than it sounds. Less, because “conscious machine” is unearned and we never pretended otherwise. More, because almost nobody builds the falsifiable frame first and then refuses to smuggle the answer into it. What follows is the record, kept honestly — the thesis spec, the make-or-break runs (including the one that broke), the digest of Eng's exegesis we worked from, and a first-person account of the bringup from the agent who was in the chair.
